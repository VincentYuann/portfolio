What changed and why

Opacity (the biggest lever)

The original is at 4%. Raising it to 9–16% is the single most impactful change — the texture becomes clearly visible instead of subliminal. Linen sits at 9%, Sumi Paper at 11%, Heavy Sumi at 16%. Above ~18% it starts looking like dirty glass rather than paper.

type="turbulence" vs type="fractalNoise"

SVG feTurbulence has two modes. fractalNoise produces smooth, cloudy noise (like fog). turbulence produces rougher, more chaotic noise with sharper transitions — better for stone, concrete, and coarse wood. The Stone and Wood panels use turbulence; Linen and Sumi Paper use fractalNoise for a softer fibrous feel.

baseFrequency — the grain size

Higher values = finer grain. The original uses 0.8 (very fine). Sumi Paper drops to 0.55 for larger, rougher clumps. Wood uses an asymmetric 0.008 0.15 — near-zero horizontal frequency means the noise stretches into long horizontal streaks, mimicking wood grain direction. This asymmetry is the key trick for directional materials.

numOctaves — layered detail

Octaves add progressively finer detail layers on top of each other. 3 octaves is smooth. 5–6 octaves adds rougher, more complex texture. Heavy Sumi uses 6, which is about the practical limit before it looks like static.

Directional overlays — the second layer

The noise alone is omnidirectional. Real materials have grain direction. Each rougher texture adds a ::after pseudo-element with repeating-linear-gradient or a second turbulence layer to add directional structure: crosshatch lines for linen, long streaks for wood, fiber strands for sumi paper.

All textures are pure CSS + inline SVG — no image files needed. Swap the noise layer's background-image and opacity in your project's body::before to apply any of these.
